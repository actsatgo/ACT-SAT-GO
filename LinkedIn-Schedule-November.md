ARTICLE TITLE: A Parent's Guide to Supporting SAT Prep (Without the Nagging)

If you're the parent of a high-schooler preparing for the SAT, you've probably felt the pull between helping and hovering. You want to support your teen, but every reminder seems to land as pressure.

Here's what we've learned from working with families across the US and India.

1. Start with a plan, not pressure
Pick a target test date together and work backward to a weekly schedule. A plan turns "Why aren't you studying?" into "What's on the plan this week?" The schedule does the reminding, so you don't have to.

2. Set a realistic goal together
Base the target on a full-length practice test and the colleges on your teen's list, not on what a cousin or classmate scored. A goal your teen believes in is one they'll work toward.

3. Provide the environment
Your most powerful tools aren't flashcards. They're protected time for full practice tests, enough sleep, and fewer late-night screens. Practice tests in particular need a quiet three-hour window.

4. Ask better questions
"What did you score?" puts the focus on a number. "What did you learn from your mistakes?" or "What felt easier this time?" puts it on progress, which is where improvement actually comes from.

5. Know when to get help
If SAT prep is causing conflict at home, that's a signal. A neutral tutor can take you out of the middle, so you get to be the parent again, not the enforcer.

6. Handle score day with care
Celebrate effort first. A lower-than-hoped score is information, not failure: it tells you exactly what to work on next, and many students improve on a retake.

The SAT is one part of the application. Your teen's confidence, and your relationship, will last far longer than any score.

If you'd like a neutral expert in your corner, we're happy to talk. You can book a free consultation at www.actsatgo.com.
